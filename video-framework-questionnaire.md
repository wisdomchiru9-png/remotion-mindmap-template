# Video Framework Questionnaire

## Section 1 - The Big Picture

### 1. Topic
How Benjamin Franklin built himself from almost nothing through attention, self-education, discipline, and deliberate practice.

### 2. Audience
People who feel distracted, undereducated, stuck, or limited by their circumstances. Especially students, creators, young professionals, and anyone trying to improve their life without privileged access or perfect conditions.

### 3. Desired Identity
A self-directed person who can control their attention, teach themselves valuable skills, and steadily build a better future.

### 4. Inverse Identity
A distracted, reactive person who consumes constantly but rarely develops real capability.

## Section 2 - Opening Hook

### 5. Hook Experience
Starting over in an unfamiliar place with no money, no contacts, no credentials, and no clear plan.

### 6. Hidden Truth
Self-invention does not begin with motivation or perfect circumstances. It begins by controlling what enters your mind and deliberately converting attention into capability.

## Section 3 - Current Beliefs

### 7. False Belief
People believe they need more motivation, better opportunities, more money, or natural talent before they can change their lives.

### 8. Invisible Enemy
Fragmented attention, passive consumption, impulsive habits, social pressure, and environments designed to keep people reactive.

## Section 4 - Root Cause

### 9. Surface Issue
Procrastination, low productivity, weak confidence, poor education, and an inability to make consistent progress.

### 10. Deeper Cause
People allow their environment and impulses to decide what they read, think about, practice, and prioritize.

## Section 5 - Authority

### 11. Author
Benjamin Franklin.

### 12. Credibility
Franklin documented his development in his *Autobiography*. His methods can also be seen in his work as a printer, writer, inventor, diplomat, entrepreneur, and civic organizer.

### 13. Specific Outcome
He developed from a teenage apprentice with limited formal education into a respected printer, influential writer, successful businessman, scientist, diplomat, and civic leader.

## Section 6 - Vocabulary

### 14. Technical Term
Deliberate practice.

### 15. Plain Definition
Actively taking something apart, reconstructing it, testing yourself, and correcting your mistakes instead of merely consuming information.

### 16. Real-Life Example
Franklin studied essays from *The Spectator*, wrote short notes about their ideas, put the original away, reconstructed the essays from memory, and compared his version with the source.

## Section 7 - The Framework

### 17. Framework Name
The Three Engines of Self-Invention.

### 18. Framework Principle
Control your inputs, deliberately build skills, and influence people through evidence and thoughtful questions rather than force.

## Section 8 - Domino Chain

### Domino 1
Uncontrolled attention exposes you to whatever your environment happens to provide.

### Domino 2
Passive consumption creates scattered thinking and weakens your ability to focus.

### Domino 3
Scattered thinking prevents deep learning and deliberate skill development.

### Domino 4
Without useful skills, you remain dependent on other people's decisions and opportunities.

### Domino 5
Over time, dependence becomes declining confidence, fewer opportunities, and an identity shaped by circumstances.

### Final Goal
Become capable of directing your own education, work, relationships, and future.

## Section 9 - Wrong Solution

### 19. Common Response
Trying to fix productivity with more motivation, complicated apps, inspirational content, or sheer willpower.

### 20. Why It Fails
These solutions treat the visible symptoms while leaving the environment, inputs, habits, and lack of deliberate practice unchanged.

## Section 10 - Framework Steps

### Step 1 - Temperance

**First action:** Reduce unnecessary consumption, spending, eating, drinking, and distractions.

**Reverse Domino 1:** It prevents impulses and the environment from automatically controlling your attention.

**Reverse Domino 2:** It creates clearer thinking and longer periods of uninterrupted focus.

**Observable action:** Protect the first hour of tomorrow. Eat simply, remove one major distraction, and spend the reclaimed time studying something useful.

**Author principle:** Franklin believed temperance created "greater clearness of head and quicker apprehension."

**Measurable result:** More available time, lower unnecessary spending, clearer thinking, and more consistent study.

**New capability:** Attention control.

### Step 2 - Deliberate Reconstruction

**What follows Step 1:** Once attention is available, you can convert reading into actual skill.

**Reverse Domino 3:** It replaces passive consumption with active learning and deliberate practice.

**Reverse Domino 4:** It builds useful abilities that create independence and opportunity.

**Evidence:** Franklin used reconstruction and paraphrasing to improve his writing. His anonymous essays were praised by experienced adults who did not realize he had written them.

**New capability:** Self-education and skill acquisition.

### Step 3 - Socratic Inquiry

**Final step:** Replace immediate assertions with thoughtful questions. Let people explain their reasoning and guide them toward agreement through their own conclusions.

**Remove the original problem:** It allows you to navigate disagreement, authority, and complex systems without constantly creating resistance or enemies.

**Why it remains relevant:** Modern workplaces, relationships, negotiations, and online conversations still reward people who can persuade without unnecessary confrontation.

## Section 11 - Long-Term Perspective

### 21. Long-Term Cost
Lost income, weak skills, damaged confidence, dependence on other people, and missed opportunities.

### 22. Modern Behavior
Doomscrolling, endless notifications, algorithmic feeds, constant meetings, short-form content, and consuming educational material without practicing it.

## Section 12 - Story

### 23. Narrative Example
A young person begins by protecting the first hour of the day and reducing passive consumption. They use that time to study one valuable subject. Instead of rereading passively, they reconstruct ideas from memory and correct their gaps. As their skills improve, they begin using questions instead of arguments in difficult conversations. Over time, they become more capable, more independent, and less controlled by their circumstances.

## Section 13 - Everyday Recognition

### 24. Everyday Recognition
Notice when your phone, workplace, friends, habits, or algorithmic feeds are deciding what deserves your attention. Also notice when you are consuming information without reconstructing, practicing, or applying it.

## Section 14 - Ending

### 25. Closing Perspective
The bread rolls no longer represent desperation alone. They represent the first visible moment of a life built through deliberate choices, disciplined attention, and self-education.

**Call to action:** Choose one useful subject, remove one distraction, and protect the first hour of tomorrow for focused learning.

**Final realization:** What gets your attention gets your life, so becoming capable begins with deciding what deserves to enter your mind.
